{% bookshop_include visual-icon name='research' %}Why Neuro-AI?

What drew me in was the chance to use computational tools to ask questions about the brain, and to let what we learn about brains challenge how we think about artificial systems. In my earlier face-familiarity research, I studied how neural representations change over time as we recognize familiar and unfamiliar faces.

Today, my doctoral question is how changing sensory evidence becomes a decision and an action. I am interested in how that process unfolds across the brain. Artificial neural networks and foundation models offer complementary ways to study brain signals. In a separate collaboration at {{ clinical_organization_label }}, I work on pediatric clinical EEG questions about ADHD and epilepsy. Explore the research directions.

I build and contribute to open-source neuroscience software because a method becomes more useful when others can inspect it, adapt it, and build on it. Sharing tools and sharing knowledge are both part of how I want to do research.

Values and responsibility

I am deeply committed to contributing to society by focusing on ethics, equity, diversity, and inclusion in science. I raise awareness about the socio-environmental risks and potential dangers of the scientific tools we develop.

A fan of open & accessible science, I want to build shareable tools that lower research barriers and help foster fairness and equity within the scientific community.

I try to stand with people who are marginalized, minoritized, underrepresented, or excluded in science and society, and to help make scientific spaces and resources more accessible.

Making the world a better place is our collective responsibility to each other and to the planet.

{% bookshop_include visual-icon name='community' %}Teaching, mentoring, and community

I firmly believe that science belongs to everyone. I enjoy sharing knowledge through teaching and mentoring, from cognitive-neuroscience courses at {{ current_university.name }} and Indigenous Pathfinders in AI to graduate and undergraduate research projects. At the MAIN 2024 educational sessions, I led a hands-on tutorial on representational similarity analysis with MEG data; the tutorial materials are available to reuse.

As a founding member of TuNA, Tunisians in Neuroscience and AI, I help connect people across the fields that shaped my own path. With Abundant Intelligences, I have contributed to research and taught hands-on AI sessions in the Future Imaginaries workshop series. I have also helped organize discussions about the societal impacts and responsible use of AI.

Land acknowledgment

My acknowledgment of Tiohtià:ke / Montréal

Tiohtià:ke/Montréal is on unceded Indigenous lands. I recognize the Kanien'kehá:ka Nation as the custodians of the lands and waters on which I live and work. During the years I have lived in Montréal, I have come to embrace it as my home and am learning about the rich history of the land and the peoples who have lived here for thousands of years. I extend my deep gratitude to them, celebrate the beauty and diversity of this land, and reaffirm my commitment to truth and reconciliation.
