Biography

I am originally from {{ profile.origin }}, and now based in {{ profile.location }}. My academic training spans Biomedical Engineering and Computer Science, and my research brings human neuroscience and computational methods together.

Research philosophy

I study how the human brain transforms changing sensory evidence into decisions and actions. I combine MEG and EEG with behavioral measures and computational decision models to examine how evidence develops over time and how neural activity relates to choice and commitment to respond. Source-level analyses and low-dimensional neural dynamics help describe the activity that unfolds during this process. This approach connects the task, model, and electrophysiological signal so the assumptions behind an interpretation can be evaluated against behavior and neural data.

Work on neural foundation models for EEG and MEG is a complementary methodological line, including questions about representation learning and generalization across datasets. I also collaborate with researchers at CHU Sainte-Justine on clinical EEG. My earlier MSc research focused on face familiarity, using MEG and task-optimized neural networks to study the timing of visual representations.

Open science and software

I build open-source neuroscience software alongside my research. I co-created and maintain MNE-Denoise, created and maintain CoCo-PiPe and Coord2Region, and contribute to MNE-Python and Jamica. This work makes analysis methods and practical tools available for other researchers to inspect, reuse, and adapt. I value clear documentation and accessible workflows, and I want the tools I help build to lower barriers to doing careful neuroscience.

Teaching and mentoring

My teaching includes UdeM cognitive-neuroscience courses and the Indigenous Pathfinders in AI program. I have mentored undergraduate research interns and led a methods tutorial on representational similarity analysis with MEG data. Across these settings, I have supported students working with neuroscience questions and computational methods.

Community and service

I have helped organize the Against Military AI reading group and workshop, and taken part in neuroscience community events. I care about making scientific spaces and resources more accessible, and about discussing the social effects of technical work alongside its methods. Teaching in Indigenous Pathfinders in AI is one part of that community involvement.
