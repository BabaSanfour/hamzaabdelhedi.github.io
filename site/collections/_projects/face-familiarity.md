## Scientific question

How does face familiarity shape the timing and strength of human brain representations, and how closely do task-optimized neural networks capture those patterns? This earlier MSc line brought together face perception, MEG, and artificial neural networks to compare model representations with time-resolved human brain responses.

## Approach and role

The thesis compared seven convolutional neural-network architectures trained for face recognition, object recognition, or both with source-localized MEG data. Its analysis focused on the timing of visual responses, including activity associated with the occipital and fusiform regions, and on similarities between network and MEG representations across frequency bands. The thesis reports earlier M170 timing for familiar faces and stronger alignment for some task-optimized networks; these statements summarize that thesis record rather than a new result.

I carried out this work as a Graduate Research Assistant during my MSc research, supervised by Prof. Karim Jerbi and Prof. Shahab Bakhtiari. The MSc work is earlier research in my trajectory. The 2022 and 2024 CCN papers and the thesis are available through the linked public outputs; the current CV also lists a related 2026 bioRxiv preprint.
