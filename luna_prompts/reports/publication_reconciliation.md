## Shared decisions

- The seven CV items absent from the baseline site were the PNPL retrospective, EEG-alignment submission, CCN 2024 paper, CCN 2022 paper, face-familiarity bioRxiv preprint, 2026 PNPL competition preprint, and MEG foundation-model roadmap. The EEG-alignment record was later removed at the owner's request; the other six additions remain on the site.
- All 8 baseline route slugs remain unchanged. The current 14 records have unique stable IDs and slugs.
- Frontmatter abstract is canonical. Existing body-only abstracts were moved unchanged for the records that retain their original abstract; the iScience record uses the final publisher abstract. The JNE record had a frontmatter abstract already, so its duplicate body copy was removed. All 14 current detail records have abstracts; the EEG-alignment record has no public route pending a primary source.
- Exactly three records are featured: the 2026 iScience article, the JNE review, and Coord2Region. This selects a recent empirical article, a field review, and a first-author software paper. The older class-imbalance paper is no longer featured.
- `preprint_url` is now an optional publication field so a final paper can retain a prior public version without mislabeling the final DOI/PDF. The CMS schema and shared contract were updated to document it.
- Relationship fields use `related_research` project IDs and `related_software` IDs. The two research documents now reference their verified outputs, and Coord2Region links to its existing software record.
- The current PNPL project page and its proceedings PDF support the retrospective as published in PMLR 2026. The PDF title page prints “PMLR 1:1–22, 2026,” but no separate volume-specific PMLR landing record was found. The record links to the official PNPL publication page and PDF; it does not invent a DOI or volume value.
- The legacy “Latest Publication” spotlight still names Coord2Region. Its copy belongs to the publication experience/UI follow-up in Stage 05 and was not changed in this data-reconciliation stage.
